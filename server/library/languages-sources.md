# Language Library editorial record

Coverage: Bahasa Melayu (`bm`) and English (`en`) for each of `d1`–`d6` and `t1`–`t5`: **22 units, 66 expanded materials, 132 original multiple-choice questions**. Each unit has one note, a separate reading with two questions, and a self-contained practice context with four questions.

These are original supplementary units covering selected skills. They are not a complete official syllabus, an examination specification, or official examination questions. Level placement follows the broad progression of the existing language bank and editorial judgement, not a claim of formal curriculum mapping.

## Editorial decisions and boundaries

- `d1`–`d3`: notes and readings use 45–80 words, simple local contexts and direct questions; later notes use 80–140 words, with readings of 60–130 words. Word counts are whitespace-based and exclude titles, summaries and questions.
- Every note has 3–5 paragraphs, an explanation, a worked example and an explicit misconception or limit, plus at least three key points.
- English follows standard written British English, with Malaysian contexts where useful. Malay uses contemporary Malay as used in Malaysia. Grammar statements are deliberately scoped to the example rather than presented as exhaustive rules.
- The modal meanings in `d6-en` are interpreted in the supplied visitor notices; the unit does not claim that each modal has only one possible use in all English.
- All schedules, prices, membership rules and permissions apply only to the invented notices. The history-interview publication protocol in `t5-bm` is a fictional project proposal, not legal advice or a statement of statutory requirements.
- Literary questions ask for the best-supported reading of the supplied original passage. Explanations distinguish textual contradictions from unsupported claims about a character's entire personality or history.
- Conditional language is kept directional: eligibility for consideration does not guarantee an award; a planned action does not establish completion. These distinctions are particularly explicit in `d3-bm`, `d6-bm`, `t2-en` and `t4-bm`.
- No specialised science, medicine, law, real institutional policy or current statistical claims are taught in these units. The numerical comparisons and survey counts are invented and contained in the passages.
