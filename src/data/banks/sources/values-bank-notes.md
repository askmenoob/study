## Editorial design and limits

- These are short practice supplements. They are not a complete DSKP mapping, an official KSSR/KSSM bank, past-paper reproductions, predicted examination questions, or a substitute for teacher-led syllabus coverage.
- Every question was newly written. Source materials were used to verify concepts, not to copy examination questions or long source passages. No paid AI API or content service was used.
- Islam content includes basic belief, pillars, elementary prayer and fasting knowledge, and applied adab grounded in concepts such as amanah, siddiq, ihsan, tabayyun, taawun and syura. It does not test another person's faith or infer hidden intentions from appearance.
- Moral is a separate, inclusive bank. Its answers rest on reasons such as ownership, consent, consequences, dignity, evidence, equitable access, accountability and consistent treatment. It contains no religious profession, worship test or assumption that morality depends on the student's religion.
- Grades reflect editorial reading and reasoning demand, not a claim that every topic belongs to an official particular-year syllabus. d1–d2 use short concrete choices and basic identification; d3–d4 add consequences, shared resources and fairness; d5–d6 ask for reasons, exceptions and competing needs; t1–t2 distinguish intent, evidence, rights and responsibilities; t3 evaluates processes and representation; t4 evaluates uncertainty, systems and safeguards; t5 combines principles and diagnoses incomplete remedies.
- Revisited concepts have different decisions and reasoning demands. For example, primary tabayyun introduces checking a claim, secondary work considers clipped context, later work requires correcting a distributed error, and t5 distinguishes copied sources from independent corroboration.
- Worship duties are qualified where necessary by eligibility or ability. The wudu scenario explicitly says the previous wudu has ended and water can be used. Hajj questions concern the basic once-in-life obligation for those meeting the conditions, not detailed exemptions, travel rules or contemporary administration. No changing zakat rates, inheritance shares, marriage rulings, disputed ritual details or fatwa-by-scenario are included.
- General community aid scenarios are explicitly about general aid, not an assertion that zakat recipients or restricted religious funds can be redefined. Restricted funds require honoring their stated purpose and seeking qualified advice where their conditions are unclear.
- Child safety scenarios direct the child toward a trusted adult; confidentiality is not framed as requiring silence about threats. Inclusion scenarios preserve agency and access without diagnosing conditions.
- The single correct option is the one that satisfies the stated facts and objectives among the four options. Ethical reasoning beyond these deliberately bounded scenarios can involve further context; the bank does not claim one universal response to every real-life dilemma.
